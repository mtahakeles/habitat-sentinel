02 — Risk model

Vegetation–heat risk estimator

Move the sliders to see how vegetation health (NDVI) and land surface temperature (LST) relate to fire risk, according to a model trained on real MODIS-derived observations from Central Canada's forest belt.

Read this before trusting the gauge: the model uses two features and is weighted towards recall. On the held-out test set it flags about 70% of fire cases, but roughly two out of three alarms are false. For early warning I'd rather over-alert than miss fires, but it's a trade-off, not a free lunch.

03 — Data & methodology

Data and method

  • Live map NASA EONET API v3 — open, keyless, real-time natural event tracker maintained by NASA GSFC. Filtered to the wildfires category, open events, fetched client-side. eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov
  • Risk model 1,713 real satellite observations (NDVI, land surface temperature, burned area, fire/no-fire label), derived from NASA MODIS/Terra imagery via LP DAAC over Central Canada's forest region. Published dataset: ouladsayadyounes/WildFires.
  • Model Logistic regression (scikit-learn) on 2 features, class-weighted for recall, evaluated on a stratified 25% test split. Training script: analysis/train_risk_model.py.
  • Not included This is not a deployment-grade early-warning system. It doesn't account for wind, humidity, fuel load, or terrain — all of which matter more than NDVI/LST alone in real fire behavior. Treat it as a demo of how far two satellite features get you, not as a forecast.
